\begin{abstract}
We show that scaled dot-product attention is not \emph{analogous} to a
Boltzmann distribution --- it \emph{is} one, exactly. The scaling factor
$1/\sqrt{d_k}$ is the inverse temperature; the dot-product scores are negative
energies; softmax is the partition-function normalization. Three independent
proofs (maximum entropy variational principle, Kim's thermodynamic Lagrangian
\citep{kim2026thermodynamic}, and the Kajitsuka--Sato Boltzmann operator theorem
\citep{kajitsuka2024transformers}) converge on this identity from different
starting points.

This identity enables a bridge: the Thermodynamic Attention Sampling Bridge
(TASB) intercepts the attention computation at inference time, samples $K$
draws from the Boltzmann distribution defined by the frozen weights, and
routes the result to thermodynamic hardware (Extropic TSU) without any
retraining or weight modification. Validated on LLaMA~3.2-3B: 98.9\% top-1
agreement at $K=10$, zero confident flips across all 28 layers and all tested
$\alpha \in [0,1]$, and a $1.81\times$ compute overhead.

The bridge also exposes a thermodynamic specific heat observable $C_v =
\mathrm{Var}_\rho(\mathcal{H})$, computable in one line of Python at zero
additional cost. Empirically: $r(C_v, \mathrm{KL}) = 0.824$ ($p = 6.1 \times
10^{-25}$) at layer 18; Shannon entropy is a stronger predictor ($r = 0.95$
population-wide). Both are architecture-agnostic: verified across LLaMA 3.2-3B
(Meta, GQA) and Phi-3-mini-4k (Microsoft, full MHA) in 30,720 observations,
directly answering the single-model-family skeptic objection.

Additional results: (1) $T = \sqrt{d_k}$ is the exact theoretical temperature,
confirmed via ablation with KL minimum at exactly that value; (2) ZOH
discretization in Mamba is the FDT-exact choice (bilinear introduces an
$O(\Delta^3)$ violation); (3) NTK-aware RoPE context extension is a Jarzynski
protocol with measurable per-head dissipated work $W_\mathrm{diss} = T \cdot
\mathrm{KL}(p_\mathrm{orig} \| p_\mathrm{NTK})$, $r(\Delta C_v, W_\mathrm{KL})
= 0.549$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Modern GPU inference is extraordinarily wasteful --- not by engineering
standards, but by physical law. The Landauer limit \citep{landauer1961irreversibility}
establishes the minimum energy required to perform one irreversible bit
operation: $kT \ln 2 \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-21}$~J at room temperature.
A modern GPU consumes roughly $10^{-12}$~J per floating-point operation.
The ratio is approximately $10^{12}$.

This gap is not an engineering problem. It is architectural. GPUs are optimized
for deterministic, differentiable arithmetic --- the exact opposite of what
physics naturally does cheaply. Thermodynamic hardware (Extropic's TSU,
Normal Computing's SPU) is built from the physics up, targeting near-Landauer
stochastic sampling \citep{extropic_tsu101}. These are not theoretical devices:
Extropic's DTM architecture operates in subthreshold CMOS using standard
fabrication, consuming approximately 2~fJ per sampling step.

The stated obstacle to running transformers on thermodynamic hardware has been
the bridge: transformer weights are trained assuming deterministic softmax;
thermodynamic hardware performs stochastic Boltzmann sampling. Moving to
stochastic hardware would seem to require retraining from scratch.

The premise is wrong. The bridge is mathematical, not architectural. Softmax
\emph{is} the Boltzmann distribution. The 1/√$d_k$ scaling factor in scaled
dot-product attention \citep{vaswani2017attention} is the thermodynamic inverse
temperature. The mathematics has been embedded in every transformer since 2017.

In February 2026, Gunn Kim \citep{kim2026thermodynamic} proved that the full
transformer forward pass emerges from a thermodynamic Lagrangian. The
Lagrangian is conditionally unique --- fixed by Chentsov's theorem on the
Fisher--Rao metric and the Shore--Johnson axioms on Shannon entropy
\citep{shore1980axiomatic, chentsov1982statistical}. This means softmax is
not one of many possible equilibria; within the Shannon--Boltzmann framework
it is the \emph{only} equilibrium. TASB is the hardware implementation of
that identity.

\section{The Boltzmann Identity}
\label{sec:identity}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The scaled dot-product attention weight for token $i$ is:
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \frac{\exp(q^\top k_i / \sqrt{d_k})}{\sum_j \exp(q^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k})}
  \label{eq:softmax}
\end{equation}

This is the Boltzmann distribution $p_i = e^{-E_i/kT}/Z$ under the identification:
\begin{align}
  E_i &= -q^\top k_i \quad \text{(negative dot product = negative energy)} \\
  kT &= \sqrt{d_k} \quad \text{(scaling factor = inverse temperature)} \\
  Z &= \textstyle\sum_j \exp(q^\top k_j / \sqrt{d_k}) \quad \text{(partition function)}
\end{align}

We prove this identification three independent ways.

\subsection{Proof 1 --- Maximum Entropy Variational Principle}

The Boltzmann distribution is the unique probability distribution maximizing
Shannon entropy subject to a fixed mean energy. Applying the MaxEnt variational
principle to the attention energy landscape $\mathcal{H}_{ij} = -q_i^\top k_j /
\sqrt{d_k}$ yields Eq.~\eqref{eq:softmax} exactly via Euler--Lagrange equations.
No approximation.

\subsection{Proof 2 --- Kim's Thermodynamic Lagrangian}

\citet{kim2026thermodynamic} derives the full transformer forward pass from a
thermodynamic Lagrangian. The Lagrangian is \emph{conditionally unique}:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Chentsov's theorem} \citep{chentsov1982statistical}: the Fisher--Rao
    metric is the \emph{only} Riemannian metric on the statistical manifold invariant
    under sufficient statistics transformations. This fixes the kinetic term uniquely.
  \item \textbf{Shore--Johnson axioms} \citep{shore1980axiomatic}: Shannon entropy is
    the \emph{only} additive, consistent entropy functional. This fixes the potential
    term uniquely.
\end{itemize}

Uniqueness means softmax is not one of many possible equilibria --- it is the only
equilibrium within the Shannon--Boltzmann framework. Kim's paper was submitted
February~9, 2026 --- 47 days before the TASB patent filing (March~28, 2026).
Kim provides the theoretical framework; TASB is the hardware implementation.

\subsection{Proof 3 --- Kajitsuka--Sato Boltzmann Operator}

\citet{kajitsuka2024transformers} prove that the softmax contextual map is a
Boltzmann operator in the representation-theoretic sense: for any energy matrix
$J$, the map $a \mapsto \sigma_s[a]$ is exactly the Boltzmann distribution with
energy $-J$. Their Lemma~1 and Theorem~2 are directly applicable to TASB's
attention energy landscape. Kim and K\&S are independent: Kim does not cite K\&S
(verified across all 44 citations in \citep{kim2026thermodynamic}), yet both
arrive at the same Boltzmann distribution via different routes.

\subsection{What Uniqueness Means for Alternatives}

$\alpha$-entmax \citep{peters2019sparse} is the inference-time instantiation of
Tsallis $q$-entropy --- a \emph{different} statistical mechanical framework.
Because Kim's Lagrangian is unique within the Shannon--Boltzmann framework,
$\alpha$-entmax and TASB provably sample from different equilibria.

Empirically confirmed: $\mathrm{KL}(\mathrm{entmax}_{1.5} \| \mathrm{softmax})
= 0.109 \pm 0.070$ across 120 head$\times$prompt combinations (LLaMA 3.2-3B,
layer 18, 5 prompts $\times$ 24 heads). Every single head shows $\mathrm{KL}>0$.
The distributions are distinct in practice, on real model weights, with effect
sizes ranging 0.005--0.37 nats.

\section{The Bridge}
\label{sec:bridge}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TASB intercepts the attention computation after $Q$, $K$, $V$ are computed but
before the softmax output is returned. It samples $K$ draws from the Boltzmann
distribution defined by $\mathcal{H} = -QK^\top/\sqrt{d_k}$, averages them,
blends the result with the original softmax weights at mixing coefficient $\alpha$,
then recomputes the value projection:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={TASB bridge (conceptual)}]
energy = -(Q @ K.T) / sqrt(dk)          # H_ij = -q_i * k_j / sqrt(dk)
p_boltzmann = boltzmann_sample(energy, K=10)   # K independent draws
p_blended = (1 - alpha) * p_softmax + alpha * p_boltzmann
attn_output = p_blended @ V
\end{lstlisting}

No gradients. No weight modification. No retraining. The weights stay frozen.

\section{The Observable: Thermodynamic Specific Heat}
\label{sec:observable}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

TASB exposes a thermodynamic specific heat observable as a side effect of the
bridge computation. For an attention head with distribution $\rho$ over energy
landscape $\mathcal{H}$:

\begin{equation}
  C_v = \mathrm{Var}_\rho(\mathcal{H}) = \mathbb{E}_\rho[\mathcal{H}^2] - \mathbb{E}_\rho[\mathcal{H}]^2
  = \sum_i p_i \!\left(\frac{q^\top k_i}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)^{\!2}
  - \left(\sum_i p_i \frac{q^\top k_i}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)^{\!2}
  \label{eq:cv}
\end{equation}

This is computable in one line from the bridge state, at zero additional cost:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python, caption={Specific heat from bridge state.}]
cv = (p * scores**2).sum(-1) - (p * scores).sum(-1)**2
\end{lstlisting}

$C_v$ measures the thermal spread of the attention distribution over its energy
landscape. High $C_v$: diffuse, thermodynamically active head, spreading attention
broadly. Low $C_v$: sharp, confident retrieval head. This is the same quantity
\citet{kim2026thermodynamic} derives as the specific heat of the attention
thermodynamic system.

\subsection{ZOH Is the FDT-Exact Discretization for SSMs}

The SSM literature transitioned from bilinear (Tustin) discretization in
S4~\citep{gu2021efficiently} to zero-order hold (ZOH) in
Mamba~\citep{gu2023mamba}. The Mamba paper cites hardware efficiency. ZOH has an
independent thermodynamic justification.

The overdamped Langevin equation $\dot{x} = Ax + \xi(t)$ with
fluctuation-dissipation-theorem (FDT) noise $Q = -\theta(A + A^\top)$ has a
unique steady-state covariance $P = \theta$ (thermal energy), satisfying the
continuous Lyapunov equation $AP + PA^\top + Q = 0$. Under ZOH discretization,
the discrete noise covariance is $\bar{Q} = \int_0^\Delta e^{At} Q e^{A^\top t} dt$
\citep{vanloan1978computing}. The discrete Lyapunov equation
$\bar{A}P\bar{A}^\top - P + \bar{Q} = 0$ holds for the \emph{same} covariance
$P$. ZOH preserves the equilibrium thermal covariance \emph{exactly} --- zero
residual, not $O(\Delta^n)$ approximation.

The bilinear (Tustin) discretization introduces a leading-order FDT violation.
In the scalar case ($a < 0$):
\begin{equation}
  \text{Bilinear FDT residual} = -\frac{a^3 \Delta^3}{12} + O(\Delta^4)
\end{equation}
The noise and dissipation are no longer exactly balanced. Wolfram Alpha-verified
from first principles. The practical magnitude is small at typical Mamba step
sizes ($\Delta \in [0.001, 0.1]$), but the structural point stands: \textbf{ZOH
is the FDT-exact discretization. Mamba arrived at the right answer without this
reason for it.}

\paragraph{Attribution.} The base result --- ZOH preserves stationary covariance
in linear stochastic systems --- is standard in control theory
\citep{vanloan1978computing}. Original here: framing as FDT preservation,
computing the bilinear violation coefficient in the stochastic FDT context, and
identifying its consequence for the S4$\to$Mamba transition. A four-pass search
across arXiv, Google Scholar, IEEE databases, and SSM lineage papers found no
prior work using FDT language for ZOH or computing this coefficient.

\subsection{NTK-Aware RoPE as a Jarzynski Protocol}

RoPE encodes token position by rotating $Q$ and $K$ vectors. NTK-aware context
extension \citep{bloc97_ntk2023} shifts rotation frequencies to extend the usable
context window. At inference time, this transition is instantaneous: all position
frequencies switch simultaneously. This is a sudden Hamiltonian switch:
$\mathcal{H}_\mathrm{orig} \to \mathcal{H}_\mathrm{NTK}$.

The Jarzynski equality \citep{jarzynski1997nonequilibrium} bounds the dissipated
work for sudden Hamiltonian switches: $\langle e^{-\beta W} \rangle =
e^{-\beta \Delta F}$, hence $\langle W \rangle \geq \Delta F$. The per-head
dissipated work is:
\begin{equation}
  W_\mathrm{diss} = T \cdot \mathrm{KL}(p_\mathrm{orig} \| p_\mathrm{NTK})
\end{equation}

If this framing is correct, $\Delta C_v = C_v^\mathrm{NTK} - C_v^\mathrm{orig}$
should correlate with $W_\mathrm{diss}$. \textbf{Result:} $r(\Delta C_v,
W_\mathrm{KL}) = 0.549$, $p = 5.5 \times 10^{-3}$, $n = 24$ Q-heads
(LLaMA~3.2-3B, layer~18). 21/24 heads show $\Delta C_v > 0$: NTK scaling
uniformly increases specific heat.

TASB $C_v$ is the only inference-time observable capable of measuring this cost.
Before TASB, the Jarzynski work of NTK context extension was invisible at the
head level.

\section{Implications and What This Enables}
\label{sec:implications}
%% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{Per-head thermodynamic diagnostics at inference time.}
$C_v$ is computed from every TASB forward pass at zero additional cost. It
provides, for every head at every layer at every token, a direct reading of
thermodynamic activity. This diagnostic was inaccessible before TASB without
a separate analysis pass.

\paragraph{Context extension cost, now visible.}
Every production deployment using NTK-aware RoPE context extension is implicitly
running a Jarzynski protocol on every attention head at every extended-context
token. The per-head dissipated work $W_\mathrm{diss} = T \cdot \mathrm{KL}(p_\mathrm{orig}
\| p_\mathrm{NTK})$ was previously undefined, unmeasurable, and unknown. With
TASB $C_v$ it is measurable per head, per layer, per token, at inference time.

\paragraph{Thermodynamic-native model design.}
The SSM literature has been making thermodynamically correct choices without the
thermodynamic reasoning: Mamba's ZOH switch is the FDT-exact choice; NTK RoPE
is a Jarzynski protocol. Both were discovered post hoc. TASB's framework makes
it possible to design future architectures from FDT and Jarzynski constraints
first --- a testable, falsifiable architectural principle the field has been missing.

\paragraph{The two theses converge.}
Thesis A: TASB is a hardware bridge --- frozen transformer weights, Boltzmann
sampling, thermodynamic hardware target, zero retraining. Thesis B: TASB is a
measurement instrument --- $C_v$ is the first inference-time thermodynamic
observable, predicting sampling error, measuring Jarzynski work, characterizing
heads across approximation schemes. These are not two separate projects. Thesis B
is only possible because of Thesis A: the bridge is what exposes the thermodynamic
state of the computation.

A frozen transformer running on Extropic TSU hardware via TASB would simultaneously:
(1) operate near-Landauer-efficient Boltzmann sampling;
(2) generate $C_v$ measurements with every forward pass at no extra cost;
(3) measure its own thermodynamic state --- head by head, token by token, in real time.

No work in the literature --- as of the patent filing date, March 28, 2026, and
as confirmed by systematic arXiv search from February~9 to March~28, 2026 ---
implements an inference-time, frozen-weight Boltzmann bridge for arbitrary
transformer attention.

\paragraph{Three properties, in combination, not found in any prior work.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
  \item \textbf{Inference-time:} operates on frozen weights at inference, not training
  \item \textbf{Exact Boltzmann:} samples from the correct distribution, not an approximation to it
  \item \textbf{Hardware path:} direct mapping to TSU via THRML, with validated API compatibility
\end{enumerate}
Any two of these three properties appear in prior work. All three together do not.
